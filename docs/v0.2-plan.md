# v0.2 plan: build like a veteran

v0.1 builds vehicles that load and work. v0.2 should build them the way a player with
1,000+ hours would: the same piece choices, structure, ballast, detail and systems as the
best workshop builds. Boats and land vehicles get equal weight.

## Workstreams

### 1. Technique mining

Turn the one-off measurements above into a tool, for example `study_references`. It reads
any set of vehicles and reports per vehicle, per body and per region (bow, stern, sides,
deck edge, roof, wheel arches):

- slope piece mix and the joints between pieces (which piece meets which, at what rotation);
- ballast location against the hull's height, length and centre of buoyancy;
- the body tree: what each sub-body is, its joint type and what it is attached to;
- components used as geometry vs as systems, decided by connectivity and position;
- paint: palette size, band structure, per-face changes;
- drivetrain and logic layout for land vehicles.

The output is local JSON, never committed. It feeds workstream 2 and becomes the source for
test shapes, the same way the owner's README and Green_Test examples did.

**Status (2026-10-19): first version done** in `swhull/study.py` as the
`study_references` tool. It measures slope sizes, pyramid/inverse pairing, ballast amount and
height, body and joint counts, detail parts, pipes split by whether they reach equipment
(adjacency estimate), paint and microprocessors. The nine references are stored as the
local set `veteran`, with the hovercraft and Marcane marked `other`. Not yet measured:
per-region breakdowns (bow, deck edge, roof, wheel arches), which piece meets which at what
rotation beyond pyramid pairing, what each sub-body is for, and drivetrain layout.

### 2. Builder scorecard

Compare a generated vehicle against the reference ranges from workstream 1. "Is this how a
veteran would build it" becomes a number that can go down over time.

**Status: first version done** as the `score_vehicle` tool. It scores size-independent ratios
only, against the min-max of references of the same kind. Baselines against `veteran`:

| Vehicle | Inside reference range |
| --- | --- |
| Tugboat preset (blocks) | 0 of 9 boat metrics |
| USS Iowa BB-61 Smooth | 1 of 12 boat metrics |
| Humvee Desert Tan 4x4 S Revised | 3 of 11 land metrics |

Still to do: show the score in the build summary when a set is stored.

### 3. Generator upgrades, in priority order

1. **Slope sizing.** Fill detail, corners and short runs with 1x1 wedges and 1x1/1x2
   pyramid pairs; keep 1x4 for long smooth runs. Target: the slope mix is within the
   reference range for the same kind of vehicle.
2. **Ballast and trim.** Place weight blocks low in the hull, sized from a target
   waterline and trim. Report the resulting centre of mass against the centre of buoyancy.
3. **Detail layer.** Add a pass that places railings, pipe rods, sign plates, slider-track
   trim and vents along feature lines that the spec names (deck edges, superstructure
   corners, masts).
4. **Sub-bodies.** Pivot-mounted turrets, radars, cranes and hatches; hinged doors and
   hoods on land vehicles. Write and read multi-body XML correctly.
5. **Paint detail.** Per-face paint for stripes, boot-tops, panel lines and markings.
6. **Land drivetrain.** Modular engine builds and microprocessor control in place of the
   single prebuilt engine, following Deimos and M998.

Each step needs a small in-game check before the next (see workstream 5).

**Status (2026-10-20).**

- *Slope sizing → box finishing (done, approved from renders).* Measuring where references put
  slopes showed the fitter already picks the right piece for a given surface; the gap was in
  finishing. `swhull/habits.py` now finishes straight-walled superstructure boxes as veterans
  do: 1x1 wedge chamfers up every vertical corner, a 1x1 bevel round the roof edge, and a
  pyramid at each roof corner, whole box or not at all. On by default with wedge smoothing.
- *Ballast (done; flotation awaits an in-game check).* No buoyancy constant is known, so it is
  estimated from the reference ships' painted waterlines: about 1.5 mass units per submerged
  voxel (1.3-1.8). Trim and GM are almost independent of it. `ballast: "auto"` (default) adds
  weight blocks low in the hull to level the trim within 1 degree, bring a light hull to its
  waterline and keep GM at least 10% of beam, capped at 40 per 1,000 structural parts. Every
  build reports draft, trim and GM. Presets with ballast now sit at 11-37 weight blocks per
  1,000 at 10-17% of hull height (references 12-37 per 1,000 at 0-21%). Findings: most presets
  were 0.36-0.56 m bow-down; five sit 0.26-0.76 m below their painted waterline, the catamaran
  worst, so their superstructures are heavy or the estimate is low.
- *Not yet:* lone pyramids on hull corners (V2 already pairs them better than V1), the
  detail layer, sub-bodies, paint, land drivetrain.

### 4. Renderer: one scene, two backends

Shapes already come from game data: blocks and slopes are exact, and components use the
installed `rom/meshes` files. What's missing is the game's lighting. The game's surface
shader is vertex colour with no textures; its look comes from sun lighting, SSAO,
tonemapping and gamma (`rom/graphics/shaders`, read locally, never copied into the repo).

- **Scene step (shared).** Build triangles, colours and normals once.
- **GPU backend.** moderngl offscreen context with our own shaders that reproduce that
  pipeline: sRGB to linear, sun plus sky ambient, SSAO, a filmic tonemap and shadows.
  Writes a PNG the model can read. Tested here: a standalone OpenGL 3.3 context opens on the
  RX 7900 XTX.
- **CPU backend.** A NumPy z-buffer rasterizer with the same lighting minus SSAO and
  shadows. Always available, used in CI and when no GPU context opens, and fast for the
  orthographic views and close-ups.
- Pick the GPU automatically and fall back to the CPU, then the current Pillow renderer.
  `inspect_view` and previews take an optional `renderer` argument.
- **Ground truth.** When the owner re-saves a generated vehicle in the editor, the game
  writes a 512x512 thumbnail next to it. Add a view that matches the thumbnail camera, so
  ours and the game's can be compared side by side.

**Status (2026-10-19): lit rendering done** in `swhull/raster.py`. One design change from the
list above: both backends only rasterize (nearest triangle and depth per pixel), and one NumPy
pass does all the lighting, so GPU and CPU pictures match and only one lighting model needs
tuning. Lighting: sun with a shared shadow map and 3x3 filtering, sky/ground ambient,
screen-space ambient occlusion, Hable filmic tonemap, 1/2.2 gamma. The GPU supersamples 3x,
the CPU 2x. `renderer` = auto/gpu/cpu/flat on `preview_hull`, `inspect_view`,
`preview_vehicle` and `preview_game_vehicle`, with `SW_RENDERER` as the default.
`get_runtime_status` and `--doctor` report the renderer and GPU; the packaged EXE finds the
GPU. Component meshes now go through NumPy, which also halved flat render time on big
vehicles.

| Render (this machine) | Flat | CPU lit | GPU lit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Patrol boat preview, 5 panels | 0.3 s | 2.4 s | 3.2 s |
| Hawk, one view (24k parts) | 5.0 s | 8.5 s | 5.9 s |
| RSV Almus, one view (83k parts) | 12.6 s | 17.6 s | 11.4 s |

Not done yet: the thumbnail-matching perspective camera; shading on the GPU (the NumPy pass
is now most of the GPU time); the interior section and deck plans, which stay flat.

### 5. Hardcore in-game testing

- A fixed ladder of test vehicles, one per upgrade in workstream 3: slope patches, a
  ballasted barge, a detailed deckhouse, a pivot turret, a hinged door and a modular-engine
  buggy. Generate each one, load it in game, re-save it for the thumbnail, then record
  results with `record_vehicle_validation`.
- Then whole builds: a small patrol boat and a 4x4 built from scratch by the MCP. The owner
  scores each against the reference set on looks, part efficiency, trim and floating, and
  driving and systems.
- Anything verified moves into [in-game testing](in-game-testing.md) and gets a regression
  test.

## v0.2 is done when

- the scorecard puts a generated patrol boat and 4x4 inside the reference ranges for slope
  mix, ballast, body count, detail and paint;
- both float or drive level in game without manual fixes;
- the owner would not pick them out as machine-built next to a workshop build of the same
  size;
- GPU and CPU renders exist, and the GPU render sits close to the game thumbnail of the
  same vehicle.

## Suggested order

1. Turn the measurements into `study_references` and the scorecard (workstreams 1-2).
2. Build the renderer's shared scene step and the CPU backend, then the GPU backend. Every
   later step needs good pictures to judge.
3. Upgrade slope sizing, then ballast, then the detail layer.
4. Sub-bodies, paint and the land drivetrain.
5. Run the full in-game ladder and whole-vehicle builds.
